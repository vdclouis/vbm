Guide

Primary vs. secondary CO2 regulator: the difference explained

In a gas installation for pouring beer, pressure drops in two stages — from the gas cylinder to the tap. Both stages use a pressure regulator, but with a different function.

Primary regulator

The primary regulator is mounted directly on the gas cylinder or bank. Its job is to convert the high and variable cylinder pressure (which drops as the cylinder empties) into a stable, lower intermediate pressure toward the rest of the installation. This is also the most important safety component in the system.

Secondary regulator

Further down the line, usually just before a tap wall or specific zone, a secondary regulator brings that intermediate pressure to the exact pour pressure. In installations with multiple tap walls or longer distances between cylinder and tap, a secondary regulator compensates for the pressure loss that occurs along the way, so every tap delivers the same pour quality.

When do you need both?

A simple installation with a single tap wall close to the gas cylinder can sometimes work with just a primary regulator. Once an installation has multiple tap walls, longer lines or several zones, a secondary regulator per zone is the standard approach for guaranteeing consistent pressure at every tap point.

Glossary

Common terms, briefly explained

Primary regulator
Regulator on the gas cylinder, reduces cylinder pressure to intermediate pressure
Secondary regulator
Regulator per zone, reduces intermediate pressure to pour pressure
Working pressure
The pressure at which a part normally operates
Burst pressure
The pressure at which a hose or part would fail (with a safety margin above working pressure)
CIP
Clean-in-Place — cleaning lines without disassembly
Rinse adapter
Connecting piece between cleaning bottle and beer line
Mixed gas
Blend of CO2 and N2, used for specific pouring techniques
